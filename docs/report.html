02 / Replay ordering decisions

SKU 85123A: WHITE HANGING HEART T-LIGHT HOLDER. Training: 2010-12-01 through 2011-02-28 (90 calendar days). Test: 2011-03-01 through 2011-12-09 (284 days). The fixed forecast is 124.02 units/day, calculated only from training sales.

Assumptions: 7 calendar days supplier lead time; 3 days of forecast demand as safety stock; GBP 1.50 cost per unit. These are user inputs, not observed supplier terms or actual product cost.

Both policies start with the same lead-time-plus-safety stock. Each day receives due orders, serves sales, loses unmet demand, then reviews stock plus outstanding orders. Weekly ordering reviews every seven days, starting on the first test day, and orders up to forecast × (lead + safety + 7). Reorder-point ordering reviews daily and orders to that same target when inventory position is at or below forecast × (lead + safety). Targets and initial stock round up to whole units. Orders arrive before demand after the stated lead time.

Change assumptions and rebuild the entire experiment:

python -m cash_planner --lead-days 14 --safety-days 5 --unit-cost 2.00 --output output/scenario.html

Fill rate is simulated units served / recorded demand units. Closing stock is averaged across every test day, including zero-sale days. There is no future-sales information in the ordering rules.

Limits to this experiment

Recorded sales omit demand that was never fulfilled in the original shop. Zero recorded sales may reflect closure or missing demand, not zero customer interest. This one gift product is not representative of semiconductor distribution. The model omits stockout substitution, backorders, supplier variability, order costs, minimum quantities, stock aging, taxes, financing, pipeline ownership and receipts after the test ends. Initial stock is assumed available; no warm-up is scored separately. Two balance-sheet dates hide within-year seasonality. Consolidated XBRL tags can miss custom disclosures or definition changes; the source audit needs human review before a real business decision.
